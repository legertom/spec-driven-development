## Talking points

- Nadia's question is the thread: "is Quill making the team faster?" Omar has a feeling; Jun has two numbers per task. Ask which answer fits on a slide.
- The week table is the whole course in one view. Name the verb for each day.
- Wait time and review time answer different questions. Wait above review means the task is too big (L3). Review above the time to write it yourself means the brief is missing a line (L1).
- The discarded CR-111 attempt stays in the table. It is the most persuasive number in the week.
- "If it is not in the repo, it is not a convention. It is a habit."
- Each anti-pattern maps to a fix and a lesson.
- Skeptics want guardrails and review times; product owners want wait times and turnaround.

## Common misconceptions

- "Shorter wait time is always better." A short wait on a huge diff means the agent skipped something. The comparison with review time is what matters.
- "Review time should reach zero once the agent is good." It should fall toward the time to read a small diff carefully. Zero is the trust-the-green-checks review.
- "The anti-patterns are beginner mistakes." The argue-with-it session and the shared checkout are most common among experienced engineers.
- "Conventions are about style." They are about reviewability by someone other than the author. A personal CLAUDE.local.md is fine, but it is not a convention.
- "Nadia needs the same pitch as the skeptic." She needs turnaround and wait time; the skeptic needs guardrails and review evidence.
