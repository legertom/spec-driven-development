## Talking points

- Open with the processor's email. Most of the room will start composing a paragraph. The lesson's answer is four attachments that already exist.
- The record is one page and every line is a pointer: a file, a number, a person, a date. Read it aloud as a stranger: who do I call, what do I switch off, how do I know the guard works?
- NIST AI RMF is a skeleton, not homework. L1, L5, L6, and L7 already filled Map, Measure, and Manage; Govern is the new writing.
- Keep the EU AI Act plain: for Sprout the floor is most likely a transparency duty. Say "not legal advice" once. The floor is not the goal; the spec is.
- Supply chain: model version, tools and MCP servers, packages, ingested documents. Fernworks lands because a PDF does not look like a dependency.
- Insufficient logging is last on purpose. Without logs no other category can be investigated, tested, or proved.
- The monthly review has a fixed agenda and ends with owned actions. Rising approval time is a fatigue signal and a security finding.

## Live demo idea

Copy the record and threat model into a dated evidence folder, then run a one-line script over `logs/guard.jsonl` that counts denials by reason and paste the output into the Measure section. Then hide the guard log and ask the room to reconstruct the A-2 incident from what remains. They cannot. Restore it and reconstruct it in five lines.

## Common misconceptions

- "Governance is paperwork." The record is a page of pointers to things engineers already built. Writing it takes an hour; not having it costs a week per audit.
- "The EU AI Act means we need a lawyer before shipping a chatbot." Possibly, and the lesson says to ask one. The likely floor for a support agent is one honest sentence in the first message.
- "The model provider is not our supply chain." A new model version can change injection resistance either way. Pin it and run the suite before upgrading.
- "We log for debugging, so we are covered." Forensic logs need who, what, when, decision, and reason on every guard and approval decision.
